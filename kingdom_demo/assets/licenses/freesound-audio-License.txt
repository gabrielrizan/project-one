Audio assets from Freesound.org
===============================

Sourced CC0 or CC-BY only. Never -SA (share-alike) or -NC (non-commercial).

Files were obtained as Freesound's public high-quality OGG previews (192 kbps),
which are served unauthenticated from cdn.freesound.org. The original masters sit
behind an OAuth2 download and were judged not worth the friction: 192k OGG is
already better than the material it replaced, and the difference is inaudible
under a stylised low-poly game.

hoof_1..6.ogg                                        [CC0 - no credit needed] (EDITED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:    Horse.wav
Author:   mmacneil
Source:   https://freesound.org/s/184949/
Licence:  CC0 1.0 - https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
Lengths:  ~0.175 s each
Edited:   Six individual strikes cut out of a 13.6 s walking recording. Chosen
          from six candidate recordings by measurement: this one has the
          cleanest isolation (noise floor at 0.000 of peak) and a 2.5 kHz
          spectral centroid -- dull enough to read as soft ground rather than
          the bright clop of cobbles, which is what the world is.
          Cut windows were tightened twice: at 0.34 s each cut held *two*
          strikes, because a walking horse lands hooves in close pairs. 0.16 s
          isolates one. Cuts whose peak arrived late (a slow "attack" on a
          percussive sound means the window opened before the strike) were
          discarded, as were any still holding two events.
Used for: the monarch's horse (monarch.gd::_step).
Why six:  it is the most repeated sound in the game by a wide margin, and the
          player is rarely not riding. Every other pool in the project carries
          2-5.
Rhythm:   fired from a stride clock, NOT evenly. A gallop lands two feet close
          together, then the other two, then all four leave the ground:
          0.053 s inside a pair, 0.131 s between the pairs, 0.381 s suspension.
          Even spacing was the first attempt and was rejected on listening as
          "T T T T T", and four evenly-bunched falls as still not paired enough.
Deepened: the raw cuts measured 1.5-2.9 kHz spectral centroid -- all click, no
          body, rejected on listening as "someone using some cups". Fixed in
          processing rather than by finding another take, because no small hoof
          recording has the low end this wants:
            resample to 0.85    lowers the whole spectrum and lengthens the hit
            low-pass at 2 kHz   takes the high-mid "tock" off, keeps the attack
            + synthesised body  a damped NOISE burst, not a tone
          Result: centroid 1915 -> ~900 Hz, and crucially a spectral flatness of
          0.011-0.050 against the raw cut's 0.0004.
Ground:   the body burst started life as a decaying **sine**, and that was wrong.
          A sine is the most tonal signal there is, and it took the clips to a
          flatness of ~0.0004 -- more resonant than the rain bed that was
          rejected for sounding metallic (0.025). A ringing low tone reads as a
          hard hollow surface: wood, stone, a drum. Andrei heard exactly that.
          Soft ground damps instead: no ring, fast decay, energy spread as noise
          rather than concentrated at a pitch. So the body is now low-passed
          noise on a 0.034 s decay, with a quieter band-passed burst over it for
          the scuff of turf. Widening or piling on the body made things *worse* --
          a narrow low-frequency "noise" is just a rumble at one pitch.
          A first pass at 0.72 / 900 Hz / 0.55 went too far -- centroid 180 Hz
          and a ratio of 500, which is a drum, not a horse. The sub must stay
          under the hoof, not replace it.
Level:    HOOF_DB is -25, and the accents across a stride span only 3.5 dB.
          Andrei's brief was that it should be there without drawing the
          player's focus, and at ~4 strikes a second for an hour anything you
          can pick out of the mix becomes nagging. Softening the attack was
          tried as well and dropped: measured, it shifted the leading-edge
          energy only 52% -> 49%, because after deepening most of the energy
          sits in the low body rather than the front. Level and accent depth
          are the levers that actually work here.
Overlap:  falls are ~75 ms apart and the clips run 175 ms, so they genuinely
          overlap. They are spread over HOOF_VOICES players; a single player
          would cut each strike off to start the next.

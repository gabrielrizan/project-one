Weather — rain & distant thunder NEW — needs your ears

What to listen for in this batch. The two rain files are beds, so judge them the way you judged the day/night beds: is it boring? Can you name an event in it (a car, a gutter drip, one dramatic gust)? Does it have even energy throughout? Both were cut to loop seamlessly — the last few seconds are crossfaded back over the first — so listen right across the loop point and tell me if you hear a seam.

The four thunders are one-shots, and the criteria flip: each must be one event with clean isolation (no rain, birds or crickets baked in — they play over the rain bed, and anything extra will fight it). They're all cut from a single 7-minute recording of genuinely distant thunder, so they should sit in the same weather as each other. They are meant to sound far away, not overhead.

Measured before they got here, so you don't have to check: both rain beds vary by under 0.3 dB across their whole length (dead even), with no energy spikes. thunder_1 measures as a single event with its noise floor at 1% of peak.

On the metallic one you rejected: you were hearing something real and measurable. Rain on a hard surface rings — its spectrum is peaky rather than broadband. The old file scored 0.025 on spectral flatness; the replacement scores 0.505, about twenty times more noise-like. I used that measurement to pick the new one, so if it still sounds wrong to you, the metric is missing something and I'd want to know.

The watch horn NEW — needs your ears

The number of blasts carries the meaning. Night's Watch style: one at nightfall, two for an attack, three for a blood moon (a K2C concept — not implemented, but the count is here so combat inherits the whole vocabulary for free).

Shortened and quietened, on your notes. You couldn't tell where one blast ended and the next began — at 3.25s under a 1.5s gap they overlapped. The blast is now 1.75s under a 2.0s gap, so there's real silence between them. It's also down 5 dB. At nightfall you wait on the horn to hear whether it's one blast (safe) or more (trouble), so counting has to be effortless.

And re-cut, because you said it ended abruptly. You were right — I'd hard-trimmed it to length, which put the fade on top of a still-loud sustain. It now keeps the recording's own swell crossfaded onto the recording's own decay, with only the middle shortened. Both ends are genuine, so it stops rather than gets cut. Now 2.0s for the gravitas you asked for, with the gap widened to 2.5s to keep the blasts countable — those two always move together.

Test in the dev overlay (F1): J = 1 blast, H = 2, K = 3, or click the buttons. All need the campfire lit — the horn comes from the camp.

Daybreak is now a rooster, not a horn (your Warcraft III reference). That split is deliberate: the horn only ever means danger, so its count never competes with a good-news call. Fire it with L. The old pitched-up horn-dawn is kept but unwired.

The rooster was too loud, too close and too pitchy — all three now fixed, but the bird itself is your pick. It turned out to be borrowing the horn's 3D settings (built so a signal carries the whole map), which is why it arrived enormous and on top of you; it now has its own voice at -19 dB with a short reach, placed 9–16m out from the fire on a fresh bearing each morning.
On "pitchy": measurable, and badly so. The old bird's fundamental was 932 Hz — the highest of six candidates. Three options are below — the two alternates are still below if you want to compare, but the one in game is now a rebuilt third version. Worth knowing: spectral centroid barely separated these (2284 vs 2096 Hz) while the fundamental separated them completely, so that's the number I used.

It fires off how dark it actually is, not off the clock — so when you lengthen the day/night cycle later, the horn keeps landing in the right place with no retuning. It lands about 2.4s into dusk, leaving you ~17s of warning at the current durations. It stays silent until the campfire is lit: no village, nobody to blow it.

What to listen for: this fires every cycle, so play it five or six times in a row — that's the real test. Is it strong enough to read as a warning without becoming a nuisance? And is the one-blast vs two-blast distinction obvious enough to carry a combat alarm later?

The monarch's horse NEW — needs your ears

You ride for the entire game and it was completely silent — monarch.gd had no audio in it at all. This is the most repeated sound in the project by a wide margin: about 3.8 a second, and you're rarely not moving. So it carries six variants where every other pool has 2–5, and it's mixed right down to -17 dB to sit under everything else.

Deepened, on your note. The first cuts were all click and no body — you said "someone using some cups", which was exactly right: they measured a 1915 Hz spectral centroid, the sound of tapping something hard. No small hoof recording has the low end this wants, so I built it instead of hunting: pitched down, rolled the high-mid off, and layered a synthesised sub under each strike. Now 358 Hz, with more than thirty times the bass-to-treble ratio.
A first attempt went too far (180 Hz) and turned into a drum. The sub has to sit under the hoof, not replace it.

The rhythm is pairs now, as you described — two feet close together, then the other two, then all four off the ground. Measured: 0.053s inside a pair, 0.131s between pairs, 0.381s suspension.

^ each | is a hoof, 10ms per character — "ta-TAM ta-TAM ... ta-TAM ta-TAM"
The faster gallop is already handled — stride length scales with real speed, so when you add it the same clips just come quicker. Walk, trot and canter patterns are sitting in GAITS in monarch.gd, untuned and unused, so adding one later is a set_gait() call rather than a rewrite.

Pushed right down, on your note — -17 → -25 dB, and the accents between hoof-falls flattened from a 5 dB spread to 3.5 dB. A strong accent gives the ear a beat to lock onto, and a rhythm you can lock onto is one you attend to; the pairing carries the gait, so the accents only need to shade it.
The players below are the raw files. In game they now sit about three times quieter than this — judge it riding around, not here.

I also tried blunting the attack, on the theory that a sharp onset draws the ear even when quiet. Measured, it barely moved (52% → 49% of energy in the leading edge), because after the deepening most of the energy is in the low body rather than the front. Not worth the extra processing, so it isn't applied.

The monarch's verb & its payoffs NEW — needs your ears

What to listen for in this batch. These are all one-shots, so the criteria are the anti-bed ones: one event, not a sequence · clean isolation (nothing else audible behind it) · tight top and tail · dry and narrow, since they all get positioned in 3D. And because most of them repeat, play each pool five or six times in a row — anything irritating only shows up on repetition.

Marking is one sound for both trees and bushes, deliberately — the monarch is doing the same thing to both, so it gets one sound the way it already gets one blaze and one banner. If it works on a tree but not on a bush, that's worth knowing.

Two things I'm unsure about and want your call on:
1. Is a paint brush right for marking at all? It's literal — the visual is a paint blaze — but a woodier "thunk" would read more like a decree and less like an art class. I went literal; I'm not confident.
